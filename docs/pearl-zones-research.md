# Pearl Zones research basis

Updated 2026-10-15. This note records the sources used to revise the Pearl Zones screen. The product remains an environmental screening interface, not a farm-yield forecast or navigation system.

## Findings applied to the UI

- **Farm-site guidance:** FAO describes sheltered sites, clear water, adequate water exchange, and about 15 m as a starting culture depth. It also notes that salinity tolerance is broader for short periods than a narrow production optimum, and that high turbidity/silt can reduce filtration efficiency. These points are now presented as guidance, not universal thresholds.
- **Natural habitat context:** Whalan et al. (2021), an AIMS-linked study off Western Australia, observed *Pinctada maxima* from 28–76 m. 91.8% of observations were shallower than 40 m, with association to relatively flat habitat. This is a regional habitat result, not a global depth rule.
- **Argo scope:** NOAA AOML describes Core Argo as measuring temperature, salinity, and pressure through roughly the upper 2,000 m on a park-and-profile cycle. Oxygen, pH, chlorophyll, backscatter, nitrate, and irradiance belong to BGC-Argo sensor packages rather than every core float. Argo observations are broad-scale and do not replace nearshore farm monitoring.
- **Pinctada fucata study limits:** The cited response-surface study tested controlled temperature/salinity combinations of 20–30 °C and 23–33‰, with an optimum around 26.3 °C / 28.3‰ for its measured physiological endpoints. It does not establish a field suitability map or pearl-yield optimum, so the UI labels this profile as a controlled-study reference.
- **Operations:** The Nature Conservancy pearl-farming case study describes biofouling as a production and animal-health risk and reports cleaning intervals commonly ranging from 4–16 weeks in the cases it reviewed. This is contextual operational information, not a universal schedule.
