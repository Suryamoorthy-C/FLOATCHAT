# Website content sources

- NOAA AOML, **Argo program**: https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/argo/ — Argo uses temperature/salinity profiling floats to measure the upper 2,000 m of the ocean.
- NOAA Global Ocean Monitoring and Observing, **Argo Program**: https://globalocean.noaa.gov/research/argo-program/ — Argo provides temperature and salinity profiles from the surface to 2,000 m.
- Argo UCSD, **Framework for adding new sensors and new mission objectives**: https://argo.ucsd.edu/about/framework-for-entering-argo/ — UNESCO-IOC approved biogeochemical Argo parameters and a framework for BGC-Argo expansion.
- BGC-Argo, **Data Management Rules**: https://biogeochemical-argo.org/data-management.php — BGC-Argo variables have dedicated data-management and quality-control documentation.
- NOAA Repository, Nyadjro et al. (2021), **Impacts of the 2019 strong IOD and monsoon events on the surface salinity in the Northern Indian Ocean**: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/48583 — Indian Ocean surface-salinity variability is linked to monsoon and IOD conditions; the result is time-dependent, not a permanent regional constant.
- NOAA Repository, Wijesekera et al. (2016), **An Ocean–Atmosphere Initiative for Bay of Bengal**: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/64843_DS1.pdf — monsoon-driven, seasonally reversing currents can export low-salinity Bay of Bengal water toward the Arabian Sea via the East India Coastal Current.
- Thoppil et al. (2025), **Sustained freshening of Arabian Sea High Salinity Water**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12006309/ — the northern Arabian Sea is strongly influenced by evaporation and can sustain high-salinity water; seasonal variability still matters.
